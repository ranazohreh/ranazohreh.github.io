Research

My academic work so far has used bulk sequencing data from patient cohorts, mostly from TCGA. I have used it to compare cancers between groups of patients and to look for candidate biomarkers. My single-cell and spatial work is on the Projects page.

Ongoing

ceRNA networks in prostate cancer by population

Manuscript in preparation

ceRNA networks link RNAs that compete for the same microRNAs. I built separate networks for African American and European American prostate cancer in one dataset (GSE104131) and tested the candidate pairs in TCGA. I am now rewriting the work as a methods paper on adding controls to a standard ceRNA pipeline, with both populations as worked examples.

DNA methylation in glioblastoma: insights from single-cell epigenomics

Review, Epigenomics, 2026

This review covers single-cell and long-read methods for measuring DNA methylation, and their use in glioblastoma research. I wrote it with Nima Rezaei and Parisa Amirifar.

Machine learning for early-onset prostate cancer

Conference e-poster, ESHG 2025

I used RNA-seq data from 20 TCGA samples from White patients under 55: 10 tumors and 10 normal tissues. I combined co-expression and differential expression analysis to select candidate genes. I then trained support vector machine, random forest and neural network classifiers. Because the sample was small, I used nested leave-one-out cross-validation. The classifiers reached AUCs of 0.86, 0.89 and 0.91 respectively. The lncRNA PCSEAT was the only gene that all three models ranked as important by SHAP and permutation importance. An independent dataset (GSE115414) confirmed its higher expression in tumors. With 20 samples, these results need testing in a larger cohort.

Prostate and testicular cancer across populations

MSc thesis, 2023 to 2025

I compared African American and European American patients, as labeled in TCGA, in prostate and testicular cancer. For each group I analyzed mRNA, lncRNA and miRNA expression and somatic mutations. I then built protein-interaction and ceRNA networks and compared their hub genes across the four groups.

Glioblastoma patients grouped by treatment

Oral presentation, CGC 2024, Best Oral Presentation Award

I compared TCGA glioblastoma patients in four groups, defined by the treatment they had received: chemotherapy, radiotherapy, both, or neither. Normal tissue formed a fifth group. The glutamatergic synapse pathway was enriched in all four patient groups. Treatment was recorded, not assigned, so the differences cannot be attributed to the treatment itself.
