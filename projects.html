Single-nucleus multiome

Brain immune cells: do gene expression and chromatin agree?

I asked whether gene expression and open chromatin agree on the activation state of microglia and perivascular macrophages, the brain’s resident immune cells, when both are measured in the same nucleus. I used paired single-nucleus RNA and ATAC data from the SEA-AD human brain atlas, covering 2,692 nuclei after quality control. I labeled each nucleus as homeostatic or activated from its RNA, and clustered the ATAC data separately. The two groupings showed almost no agreement (adjusted Rand index 0.007, where 0 is chance level). The RNA label split a continuous signal in two, which limits how much agreement could be detected.

Single-cell

Which cells express prostate cancer drug targets?

A drug target that is also expressed outside the tumor can cause side effects. Following an approach used by Mou and Harries (2025), I checked which cell types express the 20 genes most strongly linked to prostate carcinoma in Open Targets. I used single-cell RNA-seq from 4 patients (GSE193337), with 25,235 cells annotated into 8 cell types. Most of the 20 genes, such as PTEN and TP53, were expressed across all cell types. FOLH1 (PSMA), TMPRSS2 and FOXA1 were correctly found in epithelial cells, even though I did not use them to label cell types.

Spatial

Cell identity in spatial data from the mouse brain

MERFISH measures gene expression in place in the tissue. I used MERFISH data from the mouse hypothalamus (Moffitt et al., 2018), covering 161 genes and about 64,300 cells in 14 cell types. I ran spatial neighborhood analysis, then predicted cell type with a random forest and a neural network. Both reached 0.93 accuracy, and the neural network did better on rare cell types. Adding the expression of each cell’s spatial neighbors did not improve the random forest.
